Traders should be alert to further pressure on speculative assets if inflation or rates surprise higher.

## XRP-USD: developments this week

### Price weakness is the dominant signal
Several articles characterize XRP-USD as having fallen below $1 or trading near a one-year low. One report describes the token around $0.90; another frames $0.94 as a level that could be swept before potential declines toward $0.87 or $0.80. These are media and analyst references, not a live price feed. The prediction-market data is consistent with a market pricing in more downside risk than a rapid recovery: the contract for a dip to $0.80 by month-end stood at **15%**, while contracts for reaching $2 or $3 in November were below 1%.

The pricing signals have limitations: the prediction-market contracts have relatively modest reported volume compared with major financial markets, and their odds are not probabilities of a particular longer-term outcome. Still, they suggest traders are not pricing an imminent sharp rally.

### Ripple adoption is not necessarily XRP-USD adoption
Coverage reports that Türkiye’s Yapı Kredi will use Ripple Custody for its digital-asset business. This could support Ripple’s institutional services, but custody infrastructure does not necessarily require the bank to buy, hold, or use XRP-USD. The distinction matters: the headline may improve sentiment toward Ripple without creating direct token demand.

News reports also say RLUSD’s circulating supply reached roughly **$2.87 billion** and overtook a competing stablecoin. That may indicate growth in Ripple’s stablecoin activity, but the available reporting does not establish how much of that activity translates into XRP-USD usage or value capture. Similarly, coverage of a bullish Standard Chartered target for 2028 is a long-horizon opinion, not evidence that near-term price pressure has ended.

### Market structure and supply deserve caution
One report, citing XRP treasury company Evernorth, says ten accounts generated **65% of XRPL order-book volume in Q3**, despite daily order-book volume reportedly rising 26% quarter over quarter. If accurate, that concentration may mean observed activity is less broadly distributed than headline volume implies; it also makes liquidity and slippage worth monitoring. This is a reported statistic, not an independently verified market-depth measurement.

The week’s coverage includes competing commentary on token supply growth, escrow releases, and insider selling. These claims should not be treated as confirmed catalysts without checking primary data. News also quotes Ripple CTO emeritus David Schwartz pushing back on claims about the scale and timing of his XRP sales. The available material does not establish a new, verified insider-sale event.

## Macro and cross-market context

### Inflation remains elevated
FRED’s October CPI index reading implies an approximately **3.82% year-over-year increase**, while the core PCE index implies an approximately **3.10% year-over-year increase**. These rates are derived from the reported October 2026 index values relative to October 2025; they are not separate seasonally adjusted percentage readings in the tool output. Both measures remain materially above the Federal Reserve’s 2% inflation objective.

### Rates are restrictive, with a hawkish market signal
The effective federal funds rate was **3.88% in October**, unchanged from November 2025 after moving lower early in 2026 and then rising in September and October. The 10-year Treasury yield was **4.91% on 24 November**: down from its late-September level of 5.29%, but up **0.90 percentage point** from 25 November 2025.

Polymarket priced a **94% chance of a 25 bp Fed increase** after the December meeting, 4% for no change, and 2% for a 50 bp or larger increase. Those odds are notably hawkish; they are a market snapshot, not official guidance. If that pricing persists or rate expectations rise further, it would generally be a headwind for non-yielding, high-volatility crypto assets such as XRP-USD. Conversely, a benign inflation surprise or a material repricing toward lower rates could ease that pressure.

Unemployment was **4.1% in October**, down from 4.5% a year earlier. That suggests the available labor-market series does not show a sharp deterioration over this period, though one monthly measure alone is not enough to infer the broader economic outlook.

- **Near-term bias:** Cautious while XRP-USD remains below $1, as described in the week’s coverage. A sustained reclaim and hold above $1 would be more constructive than a brief move through it; failure to reclaim it keeps the reported $0.94/$0.87/$0.80 downside map relevant.
- **Event risk:** Watch the upcoming U.S. PCE release referenced in the XRP-USD coverage, and subsequent Fed-rate repricing. A hotter inflation reading could reinforce the already hawkish December-meeting odds; a cooler reading could relieve some pressure.
