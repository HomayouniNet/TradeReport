4. RSI and oversold conditions

The verified RSI is 25.42, below the conventional 30 oversold threshold.

This confirms that BTC-USD has experienced unusually strong recent downside momentum. RSI at this level can support a tactical rebound thesis, particularly after the sharp fall from $80,790.84 on October 24 to $60,784.79 on November 22.

However, there are two important cautions:

  • RSI below 30 does not identify the exact bottom.
  • In strong downtrends, RSI can remain oversold while price continues lower.

Accordingly, RSI should be used as a condition for monitoring a reversal, not as a standalone entry signal. More constructive evidence would be:

  • RSI reclaiming 30;
  • then establishing higher lows;
  • price holding above a recent swing low;
  • and MACD beginning to improve.

Until those conditions occur, oversold RSI is better interpreted as a warning that volatility and rebound risk are elevated, not as confirmation that downside risk has ended.

BTC-USD: Weekly Macro and News Report

Period reviewed: November 15–22, 2026 Asset: BTC-USD (Bitcoin USD)

Executive summary

BTC-USD remains in a risk-off, liquidity-driven environment. News coverage places BTC-USD below $60,000 after a sharp selloff, despite a rebound of more than 3% over the latest 24-hour period. The broader backdrop remains unfavorable: Treasury yields have risen materially, markets are pricing a restrictive Federal Reserve, and technology-equity weakness is spilling into crypto.

The most important near-term forces are:

  1. Tighter liquidity and higher rates: The 10-year Treasury yield rose approximately 100 basis points year over year to 5.04%, while news reports cite an 88.6% probability of a December Fed rate hike.
  2. Cross-asset deleveraging: Gold, silver, equities, technology stocks, and BTC-USD have reportedly fallen together, suggesting forced selling and cash hoarding rather than a BTC-USD-specific event alone.
  3. Miner stress: Reports indicate BTC-USD is trading substantially below JPMorgan’s estimated mining-cost level of approximately $86,000. Falling mining profitability could encourage reserve sales and add supply pressure.
  4. Technical and sentiment deterioration: Analysts cited in the news place downside risk near $50,000 if Nasdaq weakness persists. BTC-USD is also reported to be near two-year lows, although the recent rebound raises the possibility of a short-covering rally.
  5. Limited evidence of imminent macro relief: Polymarket prices a 98% probability that there will be no Fed rate cuts during 2026, while the US recession probability by year-end is only 3%. This combination implies markets expect restrictive policy without a major near-term US recession—a difficult environment for speculative liquidity-sensitive assets.

Trading stance: The evidence favors a defensive bias toward BTC-USD until either Treasury yields stabilize, Nasdaq leadership improves, or BTC-USD reclaims important breakdown levels with convincing volume. The recent bounce should initially be treated as a potential relief rally rather than confirmation of a durable bottom.

1. BTC-USD-specific news and market structure

Price weakness and potential rebound

Recent reporting describes BTC-USD as remaining below $60,000 after a sharp decline, with a rebound of more than 3% over 24 hours. This creates a two-sided setup:

  • Bearish interpretation: The bounce follows a major drawdown and may represent short covering or dip buying inside a broader downtrend.
  • Bullish interpretation: A strong reaction from two-year lows could indicate capitulation and the early stages of accumulation.

Confirmation would require BTC-USD to hold above the rebound low and subsequently produce higher highs and higher lows. A failure to do so would leave the reported $50,000 downside scenario technically relevant, particularly if Nasdaq weakness continues.

Miner economics and supply pressure

News reports cite JPMorgan’s estimate that BTC-USD’s mining cost is approximately $86,000, materially above the reported market price. The implications are significant:

  • Marginal miners may sell BTC-USD reserves to fund operating expenses.
  • Highly leveraged miners could reduce holdings, curtail expansion, or seek financing.
  • Hash-rate or network-security concerns may increase if less efficient miners exit.
  • Miner selling can amplify declines during already weak liquidity conditions.

The mining-cost estimate should not be treated as a hard price floor. Mining costs vary by energy prices, machine efficiency, financing, and geographic concentration. However, the gap between reported market price and estimated mining cost is a useful indicator of stress within the mining complex.

Network and security concerns

Investing.com reporting references newly disclosed network-security vulnerabilities alongside collapsing mining profitability. This is a material sentiment risk even if the vulnerability does not lead to a successful exploit. Traders should distinguish between:

  • A confirmed protocol-level security event,
  • A limited implementation or infrastructure vulnerability, and
  • Generalized commentary about network security.

A verified exploit or significant chain disruption would represent a substantially more severe downside catalyst than ordinary miner stress.

Institutional and corporate spillovers

The news flow also points to weakness in Bitcoin-related equities, including mining companies and Bitcoin treasury companies. MSCI’s reported decision to remove Strategy and Metaplanet from certain indexes could reduce passive demand for those equities and weaken a secondary channel of BTC-USD exposure.

This matters because:

  • Falling mining-equity prices can reduce miners’ ability to raise capital.
  • Treasury-company discounts or forced deleveraging can undermine speculative demand.
  • Correlations between BTC-USD, crypto equities, and high-beta technology stocks may remain elevated during deleveraging.

Positive adoption headlines—such as consumer spending initiatives, promotional Bitcoin distributions, and merchant sales claims—are constructive for long-term awareness but appear too small to offset the current macro and positioning pressures.

2. Macro backdrop

Federal Reserve and liquidity

The effective federal funds rate was 3.88% in October 2026, unchanged from October 2025 but higher than the mid-2026 readings near 3.63%. The weekly news flow reports that CME FedWatch pricing implied an 88.6% chance of a December rate hike.

The policy message is therefore restrictive:

  • The market is not expecting rapid monetary easing.
  • Higher short-term rates raise the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset such as BTC-USD.
  • Tighter liquidity tends to pressure leveraged crypto positions and speculative growth assets.

There is an apparent timing distinction between the FRED effective rate data and the news-based December pricing: the FRED series is monthly and currently available through October, whereas the FedWatch figure reflects a forward meeting expectation. They should not be treated as contradictory.

Polymarket assigns a 98% probability to no Fed rate cuts during 2026, reinforcing the conclusion that a near-term liquidity rescue is unlikely.

Treasury yields

The US 10-year Treasury yield was 5.04% on November 19, 2026, up from 4.04% around late November 2025—a rise of approximately 100 basis points.

This is one of the clearest macro headwinds for BTC-USD:

  • Higher real and nominal yields compete with speculative assets.
  • Rising discount rates pressure technology equities and other long-duration exposures.
  • Higher yields can attract capital away from crypto and toward cash or fixed income.
  • A rapid yield increase can trigger portfolio deleveraging across multiple asset classes.

The weekly news flow describes simultaneous weakness in gold, silver, technology stocks, and BTC-USD. That pattern is consistent with a broad cash-raising episode rather than a simple rotation from crypto into precious metals.

Yield curve

The 10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread was +0.29 percentage points on November 20, down from +0.58 a year earlier.

The curve remains positively sloped, so it does not currently signal the same recession warning associated with a deeply inverted curve. However, the decline in the spread indicates flattening:

  • Long-term yields have risen relative to short-term rates.
  • The market may be pricing persistent inflation, fiscal risk, or a higher term premium.
  • A flatter curve combined with higher absolute yields remains restrictive for risk assets.

Inflation

The CPI index increased 3.82% year over year from November 2025 to October 2026 based on the available FRED observations. This is not a low-inflation environment from a central-bank perspective, particularly when combined with rising long-term yields.

For BTC-USD, persistent inflation has two opposing effects:

  • Positive long-term narrative: Inflation can support demand for scarce or alternative monetary assets.
  • Negative near-term macro effect: If inflation prevents rate cuts or drives rate hikes, liquidity conditions become less favorable.

At present, the second effect appears dominant.

Employment and recession risk

The US unemployment rate was 4.1% in October 2026, down from 4.5% in November 2025. Polymarket prices only a 3% probability of a US recession by the end of 2026.

This is a challenging combination for BTC-USD:

  • The economy is not weak enough to force rapid monetary easing.
  • Employment remains sufficiently firm to permit restrictive policy.
  • The lack of an imminent recession reduces expectations for emergency liquidity support.
  • At the same time, elevated rates and yields can still weaken risk appetite.

The market may therefore experience a “higher for longer” regime rather than a rapid shift from tightening to aggressive easing.

3. Global market conditions

The global news flow is dominated by:

  • Sharp declines in gold and silver,
  • Broad weakness in UK and Canadian equities,
  • A technology and AI-related equity selloff,
  • Lower commodity prices and risk reduction,
  • Concerns about cash scarcity and forced selling.

The reported Nvidia decline and Nasdaq weakness are particularly relevant because BTC-USD has behaved as a high-beta liquidity asset during many recent risk episodes. If technology shares continue falling, BTC-USD may remain correlated with equity volatility rather than trade independently as a safe haven.

The reported gold decline is also noteworthy. BTC-USD and gold falling together suggests that the current market is not simply seeking inflation hedges or alternative stores of value. Instead, investors may be prioritizing liquidity and reducing positions across both traditional and digital alternatives.

Japan’s 2026 recession probability is reported at 16%, higher than the US figure, but the available data do not establish a direct BTC-USD catalyst from Japan. It is more relevant as a broader indicator of global growth uncertainty.

4. Forward-looking scenarios

Bear case

The bearish scenario becomes more likely if:

  • BTC-USD fails to hold the recent rebound,
  • Nasdaq and AI-linked equities continue declining,
  • The 10-year yield remains above 5% or rises further,
  • Miner reserve sales increase,
  • The Fed delivers or signals further tightening,
  • A credible network-security incident emerges.

Under this scenario, the reported $50,000 area becomes a plausible downside reference. A break below recent lows accompanied by increasing volume would suggest that the market has not yet completed capitulation.

Base case

The base case is a volatile consolidation below major resistance:

  • BTC-USD remains sensitive to equity-market moves.
  • Short covering produces sharp countertrend rallies.
  • Miners and leveraged holders continue to create supply overhang.
  • Traders rotate between expectations of a bottom and renewed downside.

Under this scenario, range trading and reduced position size are preferable to aggressive directional exposure.

Bull case

A durable recovery would require more than a one-day bounce. Positive confirmation would include:

  • A sustained decline in the 10-year Treasury yield,
  • Stabilization or recovery in Nasdaq and technology shares,
  • A reduction in expected Fed tightening,
  • Evidence that miner selling has subsided,
  • BTC-USD reclaiming broken support and converting it into support again,
  • Rising spot demand rather than futures-driven short covering.

A bullish move under falling yields would be more credible than a rally occurring while yields and equities remain under pressure.

5. Actionable trading implications

For short-term traders

  • Treat the rebound as suspect until BTC-USD establishes a higher-low structure.
  • Watch the relationship between BTC-USD and Nasdaq futures, especially during US trading hours.
  • Monitor the 10-year yield near and above 5%; renewed yield strength would be a negative confirmation.
  • Avoid excessive leverage because miner selling and short squeezes can produce very large intraday reversals.
  • A breakdown below the recent low with expanding volume would favor continuation risk; a reclaim of the breakdown zone would weaken the bearish setup.

For swing traders

  • Prefer waiting for macro confirmation rather than attempting to catch the first bounce.
  • Consider scaling entries rather than using a single entry point if accumulating BTC-USD.
  • Treat the $50,000 region as a scenario marker from current reporting, not a guaranteed target.
  • A sustained close above prior support, followed by a successful retest, would offer stronger evidence of trend repair.

For risk management

  • Keep BTC-USD exposure smaller while cross-asset correlations remain high.
  • Use clearly defined invalidation levels rather than relying on mining cost as a floor.
  • Separate spot exposure from leveraged futures exposure; the latter is more vulnerable to liquidation cascades.
  • Track exchange, custody, and network-security headlines independently from macro news.
  • Do not assume that low recession odds imply imminent BTC-USD upside: low recession risk may instead allow the Fed to keep policy restrictive.
