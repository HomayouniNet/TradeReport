**BTC-USD is in a strongly bearish short- and medium-term trend, with momentum deeply oversold but no confirmed reversal yet.** The latest verified close is **$60,784.79**, below the 10-day EMA, 50-day SMA, and 200-day SMA. ### Practical interpretation

## 4. RSI and oversold conditions

The verified RSI is **25.42**, below the conventional 30 oversold threshold.

This confirms that BTC-USD has experienced unusually strong recent downside momentum. RSI at this level can support a tactical rebound thesis, particularly after the sharp fall from $80,790.84 on October 24 to $60,784.79 on November 22.

However, there are two important cautions:

- RSI below 30 does not identify the exact bottom.
- In strong downtrends, RSI can remain oversold while price continues lower.

Accordingly, RSI should be used as a **condition for monitoring a reversal**, not as a standalone entry signal. More constructive evidence would be:

- RSI reclaiming 30;
- then establishing higher lows;
- price holding above a recent swing low;
- and MACD beginning to improve.

Until those conditions occur, oversold RSI is better interpreted as a warning that volatility and rebound risk are elevated, not as confirmation that downside risk has ended.

## 6. Volatility and ATR-based risk management

The verified ATR is **$2,944.47**. Relative to the verified close of $60,784.79, this is approximately **4.8% of price per ATR**.

That is a substantial daily volatility allowance. A stop placed only a few hundred dollars away from an entry would be vulnerable to ordinary BTC-USD noise. Traders should account for ATR when sizing positions and setting invalidation levels.

For example:

- A one-ATR move is approximately $2,944.
- A 1.5-ATR move is approximately $4,417.
- A 2-ATR move is approximately $5,889.

These are not recommended stop distances by themselves, but they demonstrate why position size should be reduced in the current environment. A strategy risking a fixed dollar amount should use smaller exposure when ATR rises.

Because ATR has increased from the lower levels observed earlier in the indicator series, volatility is expanding as price declines. That is generally unfavorable for highly leveraged long positions.

## Trading scenarios

### Bearish continuation scenario

The bearish case remains dominant while BTC-USD trades below the 10-day EMA and the Bollinger middle band.

Confirmation would come from:

- A decisive break below the latest verified low of **$58,680.21**;
- continued negative MACD momentum;
- RSI remaining below 30 without bullish divergence;
- expanding ATR or wider daily ranges.

The lower Bollinger Band near **$56,118.73** is a relevant downside reference in this scenario, but a break below it would not automatically imply a reversal. Traders should avoid assuming that a band touch guarantees a bounce.

### Relief-rally scenario

A countertrend rebound is plausible because RSI is oversold and BTC-USD has already experienced a substantial decline.

The first meaningful confirmation would be a close above the 10-day EMA near **$63,674.72**. A more convincing recovery would require:

1. Holding above the 10-day EMA;
2. MACD histogram improving toward zero;
3. RSI reclaiming 30 and then forming higher lows;
4. Price moving toward the Bollinger middle band near **$69,301.07**.

Even if this occurs, the 200-day SMA near **$72,324.19** and 50-day SMA near **$76,656.38** remain major trend references above the market. A rally that fails beneath those areas could still be a bear-market rally.

### Reversal and trend-recovery scenario

A more durable trend reversal would require more than an oversold bounce. The strongest evidence would be:

- BTC-USD reclaiming the 200-day SMA;
- the 10-day EMA turning upward;
- MACD crossing above its signal line and moving toward positive territory;
- RSI stabilizing above 50 rather than merely bouncing from oversold;
- price holding higher lows after the initial recovery.

Until that combination develops, the broader technical evidence favors caution.
